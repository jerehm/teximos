Every AI chat subscription has the same problem underneath it. The price is
flat. The cost is not. A customer paying $28 a month can send a one line
question to a cheap model or a long document to the most expensive model on the
market, and the second can cost a hundred times the first.

I run a small product that offers models from five companies on one
subscription, so I have had to put an actual number on this. This is how the
number works, what it comes to and the three times I got it wrong.

## A token is not a unit of cost

Model providers charge per million tokens, with one price for what you send and
a higher one for what comes back. Here is what the models on my menu cost today,
input then output, in dollars per million tokens:

- Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite: $0.30 and $2.50
- Llama 3.3 70B: $0.59 and $0.72
- Gemini 3.8 Flash: $1.50 and $7.50
- Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol: $2 and $10
- Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4: $4 and $20
- Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra: $10 and $50

Chat traffic is mostly input. Every turn resends a system prompt and the
conversation so far, and the answer is short by comparison. So I blend the two
prices at 90 percent input and 10 percent output. On that blend the cheapest
model costs $0.52 per million tokens and the most expensive costs $14.

<Pull>
A Fable token is worth 27 Flash Lite tokens. Counting them the same is how you lose money.
</Pull>

So the limit is not counted in tokens. It is counted in units, where one unit is
one Flash Lite token, and every other model is weighted by how much more it
costs. Llama counts double. Sonnet counts six times. Opus counts 11 times. Fable
and GPT-6 Astra count 27 times. Weights round up so no model is ever under
counted.

## What the plans come to

Each plan gets a weekly allowance of units plus a smaller allowance per five
hour window, so nobody burns the whole week in an afternoon.

- The free tier allows 900,000 units a week. If someone uses every unit of every
  week that costs about **$2 a month**.
- The $28 plan allows 3.5 million units a week, which tops out at about
  **$8 a month** in model costs.
- The $59 plan, which adds the frontier models, allows 10.5 million, which tops
  out at about **$24 a month**.

Those are ceilings, not averages. Most people never reach them. But a ceiling is
the number that has to be safe, because the one customer who does reach it is
the one who decides whether the plan makes money.

## The three times I got it wrong

**First, I counted tokens.** The original limit added up raw tokens. A Llama
token and a Fable token used the same budget even though one costs roughly 24
times the other. On the $59 plan, a customer who spent the whole allowance on
Fable would have cost about $640 a month. Nothing broke and nothing looked
wrong. The limit was simply measuring the wrong thing.

**Second, I copied the wrong row.** When I added the weights I priced Gemini
Flash at Gemini Flash Lite's rates. They sit next to each other on Google's
pricing page. Flash is about 4.3 times more expensive and it was also the
default model and the unit everything else was measured against. So every limit
in the product allowed 4.3 times more spending than I thought. The fix had a
trap in it. If I had simply corrected Flash and kept it as the unit, every other
weight would have shrunk by 4.3 and every paying customer would have quietly got
4.3 times more frontier budget. Instead the unit moved to Flash Lite, whose
blended price is exactly the $0.52 the old unit had claimed to be. Nobody's
allowance moved.

A price is not a fact you look up once. It is a fact you check again.
</Pull>

**Third, I forgot a model existed.** Every image a user attaches is quietly sent
to a small vision model that never appears on the menu. It had no row in the
price table. Any unknown model is charged at the highest weight on purpose, on
the theory that a missing row is more likely an expensive new model than a cheap
one. That was the right default and the wrong result: the cheapest model in the
system was billed like the most expensive one. A single question about a
screenshot could use close to half a day's allowance.

## The rules I keep now

Unknown prices are set high, never low. Charging a customer's allowance too much
for a cheap model makes a limit slightly strict. Charging too little for an
expensive one loses money every day until someone notices.

When a provider has announced a price rise, I bill the higher price now. Google
charges $2 and $10 for Gemini 4 through the end of November and $4 and $20 after.
Billing the lower price would be correct for five weeks and then silently wrong.

And the weights are checked by a script that fails if any model on the menu has
no price, because the only bug in this list that a customer would have noticed
was the third one. The other two cost me, not them, and that kind of bug does
not send you an email.
