The pitch for memory in an AI assistant is simple. You mention something once
and it is there next time. In [MODUS](https://moduspilot.com/?utm_source=teximos&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=memory), the chat app I build, memory
was one of the features I was proudest of. In July I found out it had never
worked. Not once. No remembered fact had ever reached a single prompt.

What made it hard to find was that nothing was broken in a way that showed.
Every part of the feature failed politely, returned nothing and let the chat
carry on as if all was well. There were three failures, stacked, and any one of
them alone was enough to switch the whole feature off.

## Failure one: nothing was ever saved

After each message a background job reads the conversation and pulls out facts
worth keeping. That job called a model through a gateway on its own, with no
fallback. The main chat had a chain of fallbacks for months. The background job
had none.

On the free gateway tier it was rate limited on almost every message. The error
was caught, the job returned nothing and the user got their answer as normal. In
the production logs the extraction failed on about 19 of 25 requests in a row.

<Pull>
A background job may get worse. It must never go quiet.
</Pull>

## Failure two: nothing was ever looked up

Fixing the write side exposed the read side. Before answering, the app looks up
relevant memories. That lookup is two network calls in a row: turn the question
into a vector, then search the index with it. I had given the pair 800
milliseconds.

I measured it on the live index: 688, 713, 736 and 986 milliseconds when warm
and 4,136 when cold. Production agreed. The lookup timed out on 25 of 25
consecutive requests. 800 milliseconds was not a budget. It was an off switch.

It now gets four seconds, sized for a cold start, because a small product is
cold almost all the time. It also runs alongside the other lookups the app makes
before answering, so it rarely adds time. If it still runs out, the answer goes
ahead without memory, which is exactly what used to happen anyway.

## Failure three: everything found was thrown away

Then the last one. Search results come with a similarity score and I only kept
results scoring above 0.55. That number is a reasonable bar for one family of
embedding models. My index used a different one.

I measured the scores the real index produces. A question about raising money
matched a stored fact about fundraising at 0.349. The best match for any probe
across the whole index was 0.349 and the noise sat near zero. The filter kept 0
of 7 stored facts.

<Pull>
The right memory came back first every time, and every time it was thrown away.
</Pull>

The facts had been extracted correctly, stored correctly, found correctly and
ranked correctly. The last line of the pipeline discarded them. Since the
ranking was trustworthy, the threshold only needed to cut the noise. It is now
0.10 and keeps the top three.

## What I took from it

**A similarity threshold does not travel between embedding models.** Scores are
specific to the model that produced them. Measure what your index actually
produces before picking a number, or the number may quietly be higher than
anything it will ever see.

**A timeout is a decision, not a safety margin.** Measure the cold path, because
at low traffic the cold path is the normal path.

**A caught error is an invisible one.** Every one of these failures was handled.
That was the problem. Each one was caught, logged once in a place nobody read
and turned into a polite empty result. The fix was not just the three numbers.
It was making a feature that stops working say so.

After the fixes I asked MODUS what it knew about me. It answered with two facts
I had mentioned in earlier chats, correctly, for the first time.
